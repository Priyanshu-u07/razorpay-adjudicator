[INTERNAL] Compare the two names after normalising for the differences banks commonly introduce on their own: abbreviation of the suffix, dropped or added full stops, extra or missing spaces, letter case, and truncation caused by bank field length limits. A difference that is fully explained by any of these is a formatting mismatch, not an identity mismatch, and needs nothing from the merchant.
[INTERNAL] Check the CIN on the certificate of incorporation against the CIN the merchant declared at onboarding, and confirm the company name and status on the MCA public register using that CIN. This tells you the current legal name of record without asking the merchant for anything.
[INTERNAL] Check the name and CIN or PAN shown on the other onboarding documents already on file — company PAN, GST registration, board resolution, authorised signatory ID, and the merchant's own declared business name — to see whether the bank proof is the single outlier or whether several documents carry the same variant name.
[INTERNAL] Check whether the MCA record shows a name change for this CIN, and note the date of any change. If the certificate of incorporation predates a name change, the bank proof may simply carry the newer name (or the reverse), which explains the mismatch from records you already hold.
[INTERNAL] Check the bank account number and IFSC on the bank proof against the settlement account number and IFSC the merchant entered in the onboarding form, and confirm they are identical. A mismatch in the account itself is a different and more serious issue than a name spelling difference.
[INTERNAL] Check the result of any penny-drop or bank account validation already run on this account. The name returned by the beneficiary name lookup is an independent read of the true account holder name and often settles the question outright.

[ASK] Only if checks 3, 4, 6, and 8 leave a real difference in the name — that is, the difference is not explained by abbreviation, formatting, truncation, or a recorded MCA name change, and no penny-drop name is available — ask the merchant for a bank-issued document that carries the full legal entity name: a bank account statement on bank letterhead, a bank confirmation letter, or a passbook first page, showing the same account number and IFSC already on file. Hypothesis this rules out: that the settlement account belongs to a different legal person (a director personally, a sister concern, a predecessor firm, or a trade name) rather than to the incorporated company.
[ASK] Only if check 6 shows no name change on the MCA record but the bank proof still carries a clearly different company name, ask the merchant for the certified copy of the MCA name-change certificate (Form INC-25 / fresh certificate of incorporation on change of name) or, if the account was opened under a converted proprietorship or partnership, the bank's letter recording the account transfer to the company. Hypothesis this rules out: that the account was opened under an earlier legal name and never updated with the bank, which is benign, as against the account belonging to a separate entity, which is not.
[ASK] Only if check 5 shows the merchant's declared business name and the certificate of incorporation name differ, ask the merchant to state in writing which name is the legal name and which is the trade or brand name. Hypothesis this rules out: that the mismatch stems from the merchant using a brand name in the settlement account rather than from any change of entity.
